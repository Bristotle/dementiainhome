## Research and Interview Intern, Dementia In Home

**Remote. Part time, flexible around teaching. [TERM] semester.**

Dementia In Home helps families find in-home caregivers for a parent living with
dementia. Alongside the matching service we publish a free, publicly accessible
guide to dementia care in each city we cover, used by families who are often
making these decisions for the first time and under pressure.

### What you would do

You would arrange and record short video interviews with dementia specialists:
neurologists, geriatricians, memory clinic staff, social workers and long term
care ombudsmen. We publish the interviews as a public resource, attributed to the
clinician, with a link to their practice.

The work runs in four parts:

1. **Identify clinicians** in a given city, from public sources: hospital
   directories, memory clinic staff pages, university faculty listings and the
   NPI registry. You verify each one rather than guessing an address.
2. **Invite them**, using our template, from your own project address.
3. **Record the interview.** Twenty to thirty minutes, remote, from a question
   set we provide. You send the questions in advance, every time.
4. **Prepare it for publication**, which means a short written summary, three or
   four takeaways a family could act on, and a signed release from the clinician.

A realistic pace is one to two published interviews a week once you are past the
first few.

### What you get

A portfolio of recorded interviews with named clinicians, published under your
byline as interviewer, on pages that are indexed and publicly linkable. Direct
professional contact with practitioners in geriatrics, neurology and social work,
which is difficult to arrange as a student. Practical experience of research
interviewing, consent, and writing clinical material for a lay audience.

We will provide a reference on request at the end of the placement.

### Who it suits

Students in gerontology, nursing, social work, public health, medicine or a
related field. No prior interviewing experience is required. What matters more is
being comfortable contacting professionals you do not know, being organised
enough to track a dozen conversations at once, and being careful with what people
tell you.

Anyone with a personal or family experience of dementia should know that the
interviews cover diagnosis, decline and end of life care. That is worth thinking
about before applying.

### What this is not

We are not asking clinicians to recommend or endorse Dementia In Home, and the
published pages do not suggest that they do. You are collecting expertise and
attributing it, not gathering testimonials. If a clinician asks whether we are
after an endorsement, the answer is no, and you should say so plainly.

You will not be asked to sell anything, to anyone, at any point.
