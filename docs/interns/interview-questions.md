# Interview question set

Sent to the clinician in advance, every time, without exception. The invitation
email promises this and it is also the reason busy clinicians say yes: they can
see there is no trap in it.

Twenty to thirty minutes. You will not get through all of these and you are not
meant to. Pick the six or seven that suit the person in front of you.

## Before you start

Say these three things out loud, on the recording:

1. Their name, role and institution, and ask them to correct you if any of it is
   wrong.
2. That the recording will be published on dementiainhome.com, attributed to
   them, with a link to their practice.
3. That we are not asking them to recommend or endorse our service, and that the
   page will not suggest they do.

Then ask: "Are you happy for me to record?" and wait for the answer on tape.

## Opening

- How would you describe what you do, to a family who has just had a diagnosis?
- How many families with dementia do you see in a typical month?

## The first year after diagnosis

This is the spine of the interview. It is what the pages are about.

- What do families most often get wrong in the first year?
- What is the first practical thing a family should do after a diagnosis, that
  most of them do not do?
- What do families worry about that turns out not to matter much?
- What do they not worry about that they should?
- Is there something you find yourself saying to nearly every family?

## Care at home

- When a family is deciding between care at home and a facility, what should
  actually drive that decision?
- What makes home care work well? What makes it fail?
- What should a family look for in a caregiver, beyond the obvious?
- What are the signs that the current arrangement is no longer safe?

## The caregiver

- What happens to the family member doing the caring?
- What do you tell someone who says they can manage on their own?
- When should a family ask for help, rather than when do they usually ask?

## Money and systems

Only if they are comfortable. Many clinicians will not be, and that is fine.

- What do families misunderstand about what Medicare and Medicaid cover?
- Is there help available locally that families routinely miss?

## Closing

- If you could change one thing about how families approach this, what would it
  be?
- Is there something I have not asked that you think families should hear?
- Is there anyone else you would suggest we speak to?

That last question is how the programme grows. Ask it every time.

## Afterwards

Within twenty four hours, send them:

- A thank you.
- The consent release, if it is not already signed. Nothing is published without
  it. See `consent-release.md`.
- A note of when it will go live.

## What not to do

Do not ask them to comment on Dementia In Home, our caregivers, or our service.
Do not ask a question whose answer would read as a recommendation. If they offer
praise unprompted, thank them, and leave it out of the published page. The value
of these interviews rests entirely on their being expertise rather than
advertising, and a single testimonial-shaped quote undermines every other page we
publish.

Do not give medical advice on the recording, and do not let a question invite
them to give individual medical advice to a hypothetical family.
