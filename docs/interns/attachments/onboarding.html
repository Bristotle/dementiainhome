The screening call

Twenty minutes, and it is a conversation rather than an interview. The purpose is to find out whether they will be comfortable writing to a consultant neurologist who has never heard of us, because that is the part that stops people.

  1. What drew you to this rather than a clinical placement?
  2. Tell me about a time you had to contact someone senior you did not know. What did you write?
  3. You email twelve clinicians and one replies. How does that week feel?
  4. A clinician says something on camera that would embarrass a patient if it were published. What do you do?
  5. What would you want to ask a geriatrician, if you had thirty minutes?
  6. How many hours a week can you genuinely give, in your worst week rather than your best?
  7. Anything about this that worries you?

Question four matters most. The answer to look for is that they would stop, flag it, and not publish it. There is no correct wording.

Tell them plainly: the interviews cover diagnosis, decline and end of life care. If dementia is in their own family, that is worth thinking about. Two of the applicants have said it already.

Week one, before they contact anybody

The first assignment is research, not outreach. They find and verify five dementia clinicians in one assigned city and submit them in the format below.

This is deliberate. It teaches the standard before they represent us to anyone, and it is the same method that has produced forty-seven verified university addresses with one bounce.

The rule: a real address published on a real page, and that page's URL recorded. Not a guess at a pattern, not a search result summary, not an address from a research paper. If the address cannot be read on the page, the clinician does not go on the list.

The weekly rhythm

  • Monday: assignment for the week, and anything blocked from last week.
  • During the week: invitations sent, replies handled, interviews recorded.
  • Friday: what went out, what came back, what was recorded, what is stuck.

One standing rule, from templates.ts and the content gate: we never ask a clinician to endorse or recommend us, and the published page never suggests they do. If a clinician offers praise unprompted, thank them and leave it out. The value of these interviews is that they are expertise rather than advertising, and one testimonial-shaped quote undermines every other page on the site.

Nothing is published without a signed release. See consent-release.md. The interview questions are in interview-questions.md.

What we owe them

A reference at the end. Their name on every interview they record. An answer within a day when they are stuck. And honesty about what this is: a young organisation, an unpaid placement, and work that is genuinely useful.
